## 4. Architecture Health

Architecture health: healthy.

Production evidence reinforced the approved design:

1. Legal text, operational reconciliation, executable policy, and assessment snapshot remain distinct.
2. Historical financial snapshots can be preserved without recreating the legacy evaluator as a second calculation engine.
3. Exact reconciliation candidates can be proposed without being accepted.
4. Missing fee identity blocks attribution rather than triggering name-based inference.
5. The strict Laravel domain does not need to absorb historical source ambiguity.

No architecture decision has become invalid. The primary constraints are evidence continuity and municipal authority, not framework structure.

## 5. Project Risks

1. **Financial trigger and liability policy:** The evaluator treats the due date itself as late and assigns one missed month; accepting or changing that behavior materially affects taxpayer liability.
2. **Persisted financial divergence:** The calibrated application summary says PHP 13,000 while its exact original schedule snapshots and municipal specimen say PHP 14,535.
3. **Historical fee attribution:** No persisted schedule fee item retains exact production fee identity; name signals are many-to-many.
4. **Production migration:** Identity, reference, financial, receipt, permit, and historical-parent dispositions remain unresolved.
5. **Revenue Code authority:** The specimen supports an essential-commodity half-rate in practice, but the ordinance states a ceiling and classification/rate acceptance remains municipal.
6. **Treasury authority:** Receipt numbering, due-date changes, failed-payment meaning, overrides, surcharge/penalty operation, and rounding remain unaccepted.
7. **Schedule:** Engineering can continue, but financial and migration acceptance depend on timely municipal decisions.

## 7. Major Discoveries

- Production contains a broad operational fee system: 294 definitions, 119 direct formulas, 110 range definitions, 9 overrides, and 215 UOM variable identities.
- The legacy evaluator is not a sufficient legal specification. It silently returns zero for invalid formulas, clamps negatives, applies inconsistent rounding stages, and treats the due date as late.
- Historical schedule arithmetic is coherent in aggregate, but exact formula provenance is absent because persisted items omit fee identity.
- Production names cannot restore that identity: only 13 names occur in schedule items, eight match multiple definitions, and one matches 29 definitions.
- The clearance problem is highly concentrated: five absent configuration identities explain 110 failures and each has one exact evidence-backed candidate.
- `CAL-2026-001` proves that one municipality-issued assessment, its production declarations, fee configuration, legacy evaluator, original schedule snapshots, and Revenue Code components converge on the PHP 10,710 tax and PHP 14,535 assessment.
- The legacy schedule source explicitly splits `Tax` across periods and loads non-tax fees into Q1; the specimen is an exact instance of that behavior.
- Production history shows a due-date workaround pattern: charges were added to Q2 on April 20, its due date was moved to April 21, charges were removed, and payment followed seconds later. The reason and authority are not recorded.
- The handwritten apparent PHP 500 addition has no exact application-linked counterpart in production evidence.
